### 2. "How do you manage third-party contractors on a large project?"

**FRAMEWORK:**
- Contract structure first (clear scope, KPIs, penalty clauses)
- Weekly progress reviews with data (schedule S-curve, cost burn, punch list)
- Site surveillance / independent QC inspections
- Change order discipline: every scope change documented before execution
- Early escalation of risks — never surprise senior management

**YOUR EXAMPLE:**
> "On the Sunshine project, I managed multiple EPC contractors simultaneously. I implemented a structured change management process — zero verbal authorizations, every change formally evaluated for cost/schedule impact before approval. We finished with zero change orders on the main EPC contract."

---

### 3. "How do you ensure quality for high-purity systems in semiconductor fabs?"

**ANSWER POINTS:**
- Understand the purity spec from the start (ppb-level contamination limits)
- Material qualification: 316L SS, electropolished tubing, N2 purged welding
- Weld inspection: orbital welding + 100% borescope inspection
- Cleanliness protocol: clean room assembly, bagging, capping
- FAT (Factory Acceptance Test) before delivery
- SAT (Site Acceptance Test) after installation with spec gas verification

**YOUR EXAMPLE:**
> "For the Merck TSMC specialty chemical supply system, we required semiconductor-grade cleanliness across all process contact surfaces. I implemented a full FAT/SAT protocol with the customer's process engineers, and we achieved the required purity specifications on first commissioning — no rework required."

---

### 4. "Describe how you manage project risk."

**FRAMEWORK:**
- Risk register from project kickoff
- HAZOP for process and safety risks (you have HAZOP experience)
- Qualitative risk matrix (probability × impact) + quantitative where needed
- Mitigation owner assigned to each risk
- Monthly risk review with sponsor

**YOUR EXAMPLE:**
> "On the Sunshine project, we performed HAZOP studies during FEED to identify process safety risks before finalizing design. For schedule risks, we maintained a risk register updated monthly with mitigation actions and contingency plans. This proactive approach allowed us to deliver EPC on time despite supply chain challenges."

---

### 5. "How do you handle a situation where a project is falling behind schedule?"

**ANSWER:**
1. Diagnose root cause immediately (contractor performance? scope creep? permitting delay?)
2. Quantify the delay impact (float consumed, critical path shifted?)
3. Develop recovery plan with contractor (crashing, re-sequencing, additional resources)
4. Communicate transparently to sponsor with options and cost/schedule trade-offs
5. Document everything and update baseline if scope justifies it

---

### 6. "How do you build relationships with government agencies for permitting?"

**YOUR EXPERIENCE:**
> "I have direct experience managing environmental permitting in both Taiwan and the US. In Louisiana, I coordinated air emission permits with the Louisiana DEQ and land-use permits with the U.S. Army Corps of Engineers — a multi-year process requiring detailed technical submissions and agency meetings. In Taiwan, I managed EIA processes and hold Class-A certifications in air pollution control, wastewater treatment, and waste disposal, which gives me credibility and technical fluency when engaging Taiwan EPA."
